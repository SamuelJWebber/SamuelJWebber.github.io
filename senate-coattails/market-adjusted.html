What would justify a stronger conclusion?

Better donation prediction would show that electoral prospects explain part of fundraising. It would not establish that the remainder is charisma. Independent candidate-appeal surveys can validate that interpretation. Own-election performance is a weaker benchmark because it also reflects opponents and shared political conditions.

For causal coattails, freeze the candidate score before nomination and compare close decisive primaries in which the higher-scoring contender narrowly wins or loses. Check balance, sorting, coverage and precision. Count teammates’ votes per a fixed pre-campaign population alongside vote share, preserving both turnout and persuasion.

Measurement rules

Keep the timing honest.

Download the reproducible study ↓
01 · Prospects first

Use the information available

Compare the donation window with odds dated before that window. Later election results and final settlement prices cannot serve as earlier expectations. The source audit states where the available data fall short.

02 · Support second

Keep the measures distinct

First-observed disclosed donors describe additions to the observed donor base within an election cycle; transactions describe giving activity; dollars describe resources. These records cannot identify every small donor or establish whether someone has ever supported the candidate before.

03 · Validation separately

Hold out whole elections

Use separate races or later cycles to evaluate a model. Keep paired nominees together. Do not select a candidate score because it produces the most attractive teammate effect.

Perfect calibration is assumed here

For this analysis, historical prices are treated as calibrated probabilities. The question is what that odds adjustment adds. It does not follow that odds contain every determinant of donations. Candidate appeal can itself improve those odds; fundraising networks, opponents and national attention can still affect the residual.

Competitiveness is partly an outcome

A strong candidate can turn a difficult seat into a tossup. Conditioning on the current probability removes some support generated through that improvement. Candidate-independent state fundamentals therefore remain a separate baseline; the adjusted residual is only one dimension of appeal.

A pre-primary score has stricter requirements

A market for a party winning a seat is not the same as a contender’s November chance conditional on nomination. Both primary contenders need measures fixed before the decisive round. General-election fundraising cannot retrospectively identify the stronger losing contender.
