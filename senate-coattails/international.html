The target remains candidate coattails

How do other candidates’ votes change when the party nominates the contender with greater support before the primary? A credible nomination comparison identifies that candidate-and-campaign package. Election timing, incumbency and reputation shocks answer related questions.

Keep the interpretation precise.

Donor breadth is a treatment definition

A fixed comparison of pre-primary unique donors can define the donor-rich contender. A valid close-nomination design can estimate the effect of nominating that contender without first proving that donations measure every dimension of talent.

Calling the result an all-star or charisma effect requires additional validation against independent measures of appeal. Choosing a score because it predicts teammate results would make the exercise circular.

Anonymous gifts need bounds

For Chile, count disclosed individual cash donors separately from anonymous receipts, self-financing and party transfers. Where receipt granularity and legal limits are verified, bound the unknown number of donors. Rank candidates only when one candidate’s lower bound exceeds the other’s upper bound.

A transfer row is not automatically one donation or one person. Missing filings are not zero donors. The finance audit must reconcile records before any ranking is accepted.

Within one primary, both contenders share the same population denominator: dividing donors by population or a common national benchmark does not change their ranking. Cross-race intensity can be calibrated using population, office, year and competitiveness measured before the comparison. The market-adjusted donor study tests the available historical odds and fundraising records separately from this nomination analysis.

Analysis rules that preserve the original question
  • Freeze the support measure and decisive nomination round before examining teammate outcomes.
  • Measure votes per a fixed population alongside vote share and turnout. Do not control away campaign fundraising, mobilization or persuasion after nomination.
  • Keep withdrawals, party changes and later candidacies in the assignment analysis. They can be consequences of nomination.
  • Use separately voted offices; distinguish party-slate votes from votes for identifiable teammates and from mechanical seat allocation.
  • For retrospective research, a record can describe a pre-nomination event even if released later. Audit whether later disclosure depends on who won. A real-time forecasting claim additionally requires the information to have been available at prediction time.
  • Validate a talent proxy out of time against independent appeal. Keep all candidates and surveys from one contest in the same validation fold.
  • Report balance, sorting, incomplete coverage, independent assignment counts and uncertainty. A large dataset cannot repair a missing comparison group.
