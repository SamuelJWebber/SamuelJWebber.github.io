# Proposed protocol: candidate appeal and same-party coattails

**Status:** proposed design, not preregistered. These choices are for a new analysis and must be frozen before inspecting its teammate outcomes. Existing results and the present coverage audit are exploratory. No new coattail effect has been estimated under this protocol.

## Question and ideal experiment

The ideal experiment would randomly assign a party candidate A or B for the same statewide election, after measuring their appeal and fixing the down-ballot candidate roster. Their campaign resources, persuasion, mobilization, and opponents' responses would then develop naturally. We would compare votes for the party's other candidates.

Our target is the **total effect of nominating the candidate with greater measured pre-nomination general-election appeal instead of their actual primary rival**, among close, nomination-determining contests. This is a candidate-and-campaign package, not charisma isolated from ideology, experience, reputation, or resources. Nomination after a fixed roster principally addresses effects on existing teammates; effects on recruitment before nomination are outside this intervention.

## Measure appeal before assignment

The proposed primary measurement window is **90 through 3 calendar days before the decisive nomination vote**, inclusive. Both fieldwork and public release must be complete by the three-day cutoff. Ninety days emphasizes current electoral appeal; the final three-day freeze prevents election-result information from entering the measure. This is a prospective choice, not the timing rule used by earlier analyses. Retain a coverage table showing what the cutoff excludes; do not move it to admit a compelling example. Proposed sensitivity windows, frozen alongside the main specification, are 90–14 days and 365–3 days. The former tests earlier measurement; the latter tests coverage gained at the cost of potentially stale appeal. Each uses its own fully pretreatment release cutoff and is reported separately. An older matched poll, such as Mississippi's November 2013 survey, demonstrates measurement feasibility but is not automatically eligible for the main window.

For a matched survey, compare A and B against the same opponent, using the same pollster, fieldwork, electorate, mode, and ballot options. Define the appeal gap as the difference in their **two-party support shares**: A/(A + opponent) minus B/(B + opponent), in percentage points. Retain raw candidate-support and margin differences as separately labeled measurements. Same-survey comparison removes much common electoral context; it does not make hypothetical intentions equivalent to votes or establish a causal coattail effect.

Lock the opposing candidate or opposing-candidate mixture at the measurement cutoff. If the opposing party has already nominated, use that nominee. If its nomination is unresolved, include every ballot-qualified opposing candidate known at the cutoff and use prespecified ex-ante weights: normalized mean primary-poll support in the same window, or equal weights if none exist. Every weighted opponent must have a matched A/B comparison; otherwise the direct appeal measure is missing. Do not choose the eventual opposing nominee retrospectively, drop an inconvenient opponent, or renormalize over only the available matchups. This matters for simultaneous primaries: a Colorado Republican's relative appeal against Bennet cannot be selected using Bennet's later victory over Romanoff. Report the weights and their information dates. Pool repeated matched surveys within opponent first, with equal weight per independent survey; do not count duplicate releases as independent evidence.

These vote-intention contrasts can miss differences in organizational mobilization and in willingness to vote at all. They define comparative stated electoral appeal, not every dimension of exceptional campaigning. Validate the score against separate own-election vote and participation outcomes without using teammate outcomes or requiring a significant result to retain a contest. Registered-voter or all-eligible samples form the main measurement tier; likely-voter samples form a separate tier because screening can omit potential mobilization. Record sample size, sponsor, wording, question order, undecideds, and survey dates. Call a survey randomized only when its documentation establishes randomized question assignment or order. For paired questions, use respondent-level covariance when available. Without it, report sensitivity to plausible covariance or a conservative uncertainty bound rather than treating the questions as independent. Propagate polling and opponent-weight uncertainty into candidate ranking and estimates. Keep weakly distinguished pairs visible; do not silently treat a noisy positive point estimate as proof of an all-star. A continuous appeal gap is preferable to choosing an outcome-optimized star threshold.

## Construct the assignment cohort first

Build the full Senate/governor nomination roster from official returns before joining polls, ideology, or donations. For conventional nominations, use the **decisive final round**, including final runoffs. A first-round threshold that merely triggers a runoff is a different treatment. Exclude or separately specify nonpartisan top-two systems, conventions, ranked-choice rules, and unusual nomination procedures based on their rules, not their later outcomes.

Orient the running variable as the higher-measured-appeal candidate's decisive vote margin over the rival, divided by their combined votes. The proposed descriptive close-election window is ±5 points; show ±2.5 and ±10 as prespecified sensitivities. These windows are not assertions of random assignment. For multiperson races, audit whether the leading pair is separated from third place and flag multidimensional near-ties. Fix the inferential method and its bandwidth/window rule before outcome analysis; do not search across windows or estimators for a favorable result.

Nomination is the treatment. Retain close primary losers who subsequently run independently or as write-ins, nominees who withdraw, and other noncompliance in the **nomination intention-to-treat** analysis. Their later conduct may be a consequence of nomination. Any analysis of actual general-election ballot identity requires a separately justified treatment definition; a fuzzy design is not automatically valid. Resolve candidate identifiers against official records and document missing or conflicting returns.

Publish counts at every step: full roster, eligible rules, decisive near-ties, measured appeal, observed outcomes, and final analysis. Poll coverage selects salient races; complete cases therefore identify a narrower population even if their within-sample assignment is credible. Compare covered and uncovered contests on pretreatment features. Weighting or imputation cannot recover representativeness without additional assumptions or repair selection caused by outcome-dependent source availability.

## Outcomes and identification checks

The main outcome is same-party **U.S. House votes per fixed pre-election eligible population**, aggregated within the state. Use the latest population vintage published before nomination, with its reference period also before nomination. A later-released estimate of a pretreatment population belongs in a separately labeled sensitivity analysis. Report same-party two-party House vote share alongside it. Analyze a concurrent governor and state-legislative outcomes separately, with appropriate ballot-opportunity denominators. Do not count an absent race as zero or multiply the number of independent assignments by expanding to districts or precincts. Preserve unopposed races and candidate absences under rules fixed from pretreatment information, with transparent sensitivity analyses.

The vote-count rate includes mobilization and persuasion; shares capture electoral competition. Neither by itself identifies a mechanism. Changing electorate composition means that an aggregate share change is not a pure persuasion estimate. Turnout, roll-off, and validated voter participation can supply secondary mechanism evidence. Do not control away postnomination turnout, fundraising, campaign spending, teammate resources, or the focal candidate's own result.

Identification requires potential teammate outcomes to vary smoothly through the decisive nomination threshold, or a defensible local-randomization approximation. Assess institutional manipulation risks, density, party composition, prior presidential support, prior teammate results, demographics, and other fixed characteristics. Use party and calendar-cycle adjustment, with party-by-cycle blocking/interactions only where the cohort has adequate support; disclose sparse cells and overlap. Prior outcomes improve precision and diagnose imbalance. **They, party-cycle controls, and a nonsignificant balance test do not create randomization.** Large unexplained discontinuities or lack of overlap prevent a headline causal interpretation. Cluster uncertainty at least by state and use inference appropriate to the small number of independent assignments.

## Donors as an independently validated proxy

The prediction target is the **within-survey A-versus-B general-election appeal gap**, never teammates' election results. Candidate features can include pretreatment unique donor breadth, in-state breadth, repeat-giver fraction, and early growth. Audit identity deduplication, disclosure thresholds, receipts versus publicly available filing dates, and coverage changes. Within one state-primary pair, dividing both donor counts by the same population does not change their ranking.

Build the prediction training set from all eligible hypothetical-matchup contests, including non-close primaries; the small causal-analysis subset need not be the entire training set. Train and validate out of time, keeping both candidates, every poll, and every opponent matchup from one contest in the same fold. Fit preprocessing and tuning only within training data; reserve later cycles for final evaluation. A held-out contest's later election performance, amended-after-cutoff data, and teammate outcomes cannot enter the score. Freeze the selected model before the causal analysis and propagate its prediction uncertainty.

Report direct-poll and model-imputed cohorts separately. Good out-of-time prediction validates a measurement proxy, **not causal identification**. Poor prediction means the broader treatment must remain labeled donor support, not general-election appeal. Imputation may expand coverage but inherits transport assumptions from unusually polled races and cannot erase their selection bias.

## Decision and precision limits

Report an inconclusive result when quality contrasts are poorly measured, the design fails its assignment diagnostics, coverage is too selective for the claimed population, or intervals cannot distinguish substantively important help from harm. Do not require a significant focal-candidate own-vote effect to include a contest; use that outcome as independent validation, with its uncertainty reported. Show all prespecified sensitivities, including unfavorable ones.

The existing donor analysis has an adjusted House standard error of about **3.36 percentage points** and only **32 comparisons across 19 states**. A rough 80%-power minimum detectable effect is **9–10 points**, depending on the small-sample approximation. This is far from reliably detecting a 1–2-point coattail. Newly recovered contests are potential assignments, not automatically eligible observations, and adding district rows does not solve that problem. As an illustrative benchmark, an ideal balanced randomized experiment with residual outcome SD of 3–5 points needs roughly 71–196 independent assignments to detect a 2-point effect at 80% power and a two-sided 5% level; this is not a forecast for the RD.

Even a successful analysis estimates a local nomination effect among competitive primaries with measurable appeal differences. It does not, without further evidence, identify an individual Obama/Ossoff effect, the effect of an uncontested superstar, or a universal conversion rate from own-candidate support into teammate votes.
