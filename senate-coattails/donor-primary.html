What this comparison means

It estimates a difference associated with narrowly nominating the actual broader-support contender instead of their actual rival. It does not identify an intrinsic charisma effect, a universal all-star multiplier, or Ossoff’s individual contribution.

Does the donor definition work?

The nominee’s own result is reported separately as a relevance check. More donors does not automatically mean stronger general-election appeal.

Does prior party strength still confound it?

The identifying claim comes from comparable close nominations, not from subtracting last election’s result. Prior outcomes are balance checks and precision controls. Adjusting for them cannot guarantee that the comparison is causal.

Download code, aggregate data & protocol ↓
01

Define support without teammate results

The main measure is national unique disclosed donor identities. In-state donors and donors making individual gifts above $200 or $500 are separate sensitivity checks. Within a primary, both contenders face the same population denominator; per-capita scaling does not change their rank.

02

Change the nominee at a close threshold

The main comparison uses top-two primary margins below five points, separate linear trends on each side and greater weight on closer contests. Two- and ten-point windows are fixed checks. A 51–49 primary has a two-point margin.

03

Measure the whole candidacy

House outcomes average the party’s share across contested districts, giving each nomination one observation. Governor results require a concurrent election. Current turnout, campaign spending and the Senate result are not controls in the teammate model.

Important limits on what the results establish

Relative support is not an all-star classification. More disclosed donors than one rival does not mean nationally exceptional talent. The own-Senate estimate tests whether this contrast translates into general-election strength. The definition is not retuned to make coattails positive.

Retrospective disclosure. Receipts are dated before the primary, but the downloaded records include later filings and amendments. A late filing can disclose an earlier gift. The bulk subset’s thresholds also change over time: $500 reporting-period gifts in 1975–1988, $200 in 1989–2014, and over $200 cumulatively from 2015. The larger-gift checks do not remove every reporting risk. All 289 referenced filing IDs were checked; 65 of 130 candidate appearances have complete filing-date coverage for retained records. This still does not recover superseded original filings.

Selected historical sample. The 65 audited pairs come from a prior research dataset with observed pre-primary money shares, not a census of all close primaries. All mapped candidates receive the new donor-count measure. Runoff-rule states and nonpartisan top-two/top-four systems were conservatively excluded. Independently checked nomination margins and remaining provisional records are flagged in the download.

Conditional uncertainty. Intervals cluster errors by state and use a small-cluster t critical value. They are conditional on these models, not bias-corrected regression-discontinuity intervals. Sparse clusters, a narrow support range, unresolved balance and measurement error limit causal interpretation. A wide interval crossing zero does not establish that coattails are absent.

House coverage. The main House endpoint uses districts with observed positive votes for both major parties. Changes in which districts are contested can affect that average. Vermont 2000 is omitted from the adjusted models because the prior Democratic House share is unavailable; its current D/R share also excludes a dominant independent candidate. Volume endpoints require complete statewide reporting; they do not silently fill absent party votes with zero. District-level observations are not treated as independent nomination experiments.

Timing of specification. The protocol was written before new donor ranks and current outcomes were combined. Earlier studies and their diagnostics were already known. This is retrospective analysis, not an externally preregistered experiment.

Sources and reproducibility

Individual contributions and candidate/committee links: Federal Election Commission; see its bulk-file definitions. Primary source cohort: Myers replication data and Algara and coauthors, checked against official primary dates and returns where available.

General-election returns: MIT Election Data and Science Lab Senate and presidential files, DeLuca House returns, and official state governor returns. Population: strictly prior Census counts. The download includes source URLs, hashes, definitions, exclusions, exact results and executable analysis. An independent MIT House audit leaves main-sample source differences below 0.045 percentage points after resolving a party-label issue; wider-sample discrepancies are documented in the bundle. Raw individual donor identities are not published.
