How it works

Open the app, point your camera at the car, and tap the button that matches the problem. TattleTale reads the license plate and works out the block from your GPS, using the city's own street map stored in the app. It then opens a text message to NYC 311's text line (311-692) with the complaint written out. You press Send, and a 311 agent files it with NYPD.

Photos you took earlier work too. "Choose a photo" reads the time and GPS the camera saved in the photo. On Android it opens the Files picker, because Android's Photo Picker removes the location before a web app sees it. Fastest: install TattleTale (Chrome menu, Install app), then share a photo to it straight from the camera or gallery and tap what's wrong; the text to 311 opens. Sharing keeps the time. If Android removed the location, TattleTale uses where you are now for a photo taken in the last 15 minutes, or reads the street signs in the photo.

If you prefer the 311 website, turn on "Let me check before texting" and choose the website option. Every report is saved on your phone under My reports so you can track what happened.

AI assist (optional)

Uses Jev, TypeSafe's decision model, on photos you choose or share: it picks what the car is doing (and only when it's at least 80% sure), finds the plate when the phone can't, and double-checks shaky plate readings. Those photos go to TypeSafe through OpenRouter. It runs on your own OpenRouter key, costs a fraction of a cent per photo, and the key stays on this phone. Set a spending limit on the key.

Privacy

Unless you turn on AI assist, the photo, plate reading and address lookup never leave your phone. The only outside requests are the text you choose to send and, on the Stats page, a query to NYC Open Data for complaints near a point rounded to about 100 meters.

Limits

311 has no public submission API, so the last step is always yours: pressing Send, or Submit on the 311 website. The plate reader can misread dirty, angled or far-away plates; the text shows the plate so you can fix it before sending. The idling reward needs a DEP account and a time-stamped video, so it cannot be one tap.
