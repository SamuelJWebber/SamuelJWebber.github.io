PRICE AND PAYOUT

How the adjustment works

Forecast accuracy

The midpoint is halfway between the highest bid and lowest ask. It removes the choice of which edge of the spread to use as the forecast. It can still be biased, especially in a thin market. We pick the favorite from the midpoint and require at least 51%, then score each available price against the same outcomes.

Proportional no-vig price

The two buy prices can sum to more than $1. This method divides the favorite’s ask by the sum of the YES and NO asks. It allocates the excess in proportion to probability. The midpoint instead splits the spread evenly in cents. Both are estimates of the forecast inside the spread.

Buyer economics

For a contract that pays $1 if the favorite wins, gross profit per contract is its payout minus the quoted ask. Subtract an assumed fee to see how much of the return it would absorb. These quotes give a benchmark for a small order held to settlement. Actual fills, size, and historical fees can differ.

METHODOLOGY

How to read the results

Which side counts

Each binary contract contributes the outcome with the higher quoted chance. A 30% quote for one outcome is a 70% quote for its opposite. A forecast qualifies at a given horizon only if the favorite is priced at 51% or higher. For a matched comparison it must qualify at both dates.

Prices, spreads and fees

Accuracy compares outcomes with the recorded price. Most archives supply trades or closes. Manifold supplies the probability after a trade. When the opposite side was favored, its price is inferred as 1 minus the recorded price. The main accuracy charts use these recorded prices directly. The Spreads & fees view uses separate historical quote samples to compare the trade, midpoint and ask on the same contracts. Kalshi order book example.

Exact probability and signed bias

The default lookup, chart and table use exact probabilities. You can include fractional quotes in the table or choose a ± margin in the lookup. Signed bias equals the actual favorite win rate minus the mean forecast. A positive value means more favorites won than their prices implied. Errors in opposite directions can cancel in this average. The Brier score measures error contract by contract.

Forecast accuracy

The favorite win rate measures how often the higher-priced side paid out. The Brier score is the mean squared difference between price and the 0-or-1 result. Smaller is better. A forecast of 50% on every question has a Brier score of 0.25.

Which prices qualify

Each horizon includes only contracts with an eligible observation for that day under the source rules. We never interpolate between days. A contract can qualify at day 7 and be absent at day 30. The details above identify what clock and price each source uses.

Rare outcomes

Compare observed and expected upsets as well as percentage-point gaps. If 1.5% of longshots win against a 1% forecast, the absolute error is 0.5 points and the upset rate is 50% higher than predicted. A handful of wins can move these estimates substantially.

Counting events

Each contract gets equal weight once per day. Several contracts can settle on the same race, game or release. Event counts show distinct parent events where available. Cross-venue statistics can differ because of what each venue lists and when it trades.
