# Candidate audit: strongest next NYC lane-conversion study

The best new pilot is McGuinness Boulevard's 2023–2026 staged redesign, with Queens Boulevard between Skillman and Roosevelt Avenues as a second modern candidate. This is a research-priority ranking. Neither is yet a demonstrated natural experiment, and finding a better corridor does not supply missing passenger counts or a valid counterfactual.

## What the treatment actually is

McGuinness has three distinct interventions:

1. The northern section between Calyer Street and the Pulaski approach received bike protection in October 2023. Contemporaneous DOT comments document two moving lanes per direction from 07:00 to 19:00 and one moving lane overnight. These comments are in the September 21, 2023 District Service Cabinet attachment, physical pages 138–140 of the October 11 CB1 minutes PDF. Weekday/weekend signage and actual compliance still need confirmation.
2. Southern work between Calyer and Meeker began in late October 2024, with major restriping scheduled from November 4. The design provides one moving lane, parking/loading and protected cycling space each direction. A start notice is not the exact day every block became operational.
3. Northern work announced May 27, 2026 extends the permanent one-moving-lane configuration. This is an incremental road-capacity and curb-management change in a corridor that already has a bicycle facility. It cannot be described as first installation of a bike lane. The April presentation focuses on Calyer–Freeman; broad press descriptions reach the Pulaski approach. Code exact blocks, not the press-release shorthand.

A September 2024 presentation corroborates the 07:00–19:00 rule but also describes a then-proposed continuation. The contemporaneous northern minutes are more direct evidence. Do not substitute “rush hours” for the actual twelve-hour daytime regime.

## How to use this variation

First build an external comparison network whose pre-period traffic demand and travel-time patterns match the treated catchment. Treat adjacent and connecting streets as spillover outcomes. The northern section is not a clean control for the south: many vehicles traverse both. Likewise, Queens Boulevard is not a control for McGuinness; both changed in autumn 2024 and share Queensboro approach traffic.

The 2026 daytime-versus-overnight contrast can sharpen a limited question: what happened when the extra daytime moving lane disappeared, conditional on the existing bikeway? Estimate the change in that contrast relative to the same contrast in external controls. It is not random assignment. Nighttime parking, delivery activity, enforcement, visibility and the demand curve differ. Check many pre-periods, placebo dates and unaffected clock hours; report the incremental estimand separately from the full conversion package.

The 2024 southern intervention is the better substantive test of creating a protected cycling route while permanently reducing vehicle lanes. The new April 2026 evaluation supplies unusually useful count, travel-time and diversion evidence. Its raw survey dates, locations and units must pass an alignment audit before combining modes. The separate evaluation extraction contains that audit.

## Runner-up and other useful cases

Queens Boulevard's 2024 Skillman–Roosevelt project explicitly changes four full-time lanes to three each direction and removes rush-hour curb regulations. The implementation flyer says September 2024; the November 12 release certifies completion. It is misleadingly listed on the 2025 project archive. This makes it a strong second candidate for ATR and local E-ZPass coverage checks, and an example of why archive years are unsafe treatment dates.

Tenth/Amsterdam West 52–72 in 2019 is the best pre-pandemic extension candidate: a true full-time lane reduction, dense taxi and Citi Bike observations, and potential 60th Street screenline records. Amsterdam West 72–110 in 2016 and West 110–162 in 2019 expand the cohort, but bike-share expansion and sparse all-bike counts matter.

Skillman/43rd in 2018 has valuable variation: some low-volume blocks lose a lane while higher-volume blocks retain two. This is deliberate assignment based on traffic volume, not a natural experiment. Code the actual segment-level treatment; do not count every protected-bike block as a lane removal.

Seagirt in 2023 is a verified modern road diet with strong seasonal-demand concerns. Bailey/Sedgwick is already built by October 2026, despite a current-project page still describing a proposal. West 72nd Street may offer prospective measurement, but its current construction status and final lane geometry must be verified first.

## Executable pilot and stopping rules

- Fix the geographic catchment and direction-specific screenline before inspecting post-treatment results. Count all parallel routes simultaneously, with explicit boundaries and no double-counting of people who transfer modes.
- Obtain per-block striping/sign operating dates, the old and new lane schedules, and a construction exclusion period.
- Spatially join ATR sites and local E-ZPass links; require repeated comparable windows before and after. Missing samples are not zeros. Probe travel times are not traffic volumes.
- Use Citi Bike and TLC OD/time records as measured subsets. Neither establishes physical routes or all-person flow. Station entries are not subway passenger loads across the chosen cut.
- Select external controls using pre-period outcomes only. Exclude concurrently treated corridors and likely diversion corridors from the control pool. Test pre-trend equivalence, placebo dates, donor sensitivity and the impact of major transit/traffic-policy changes.
- If aligned passenger counts remain missing, report a partial outcome such as bicycle counts or probe travel time. Do not relabel it as total people moved or door-to-door person-minutes.

No credible instrument was found. Weather directly changes cycling and travel; political timing may coincide with other transport policies. Randomizing future rollout would require agency cooperation and multiple eligible corridors. A defensible observational analysis is achievable, but its causal claim must track these assumptions and measurement limits.
