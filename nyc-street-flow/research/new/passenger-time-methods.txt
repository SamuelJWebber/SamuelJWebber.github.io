MTA passenger-time supplement

Sum published total_apt and total_att over disjoint month/line/period rows, divided by summed num_passengers for the average.
Within the same line/month/period cells present in every selected year, weight each year’s APT and ATT means by the corresponding 2019 num_passengers, and divide by total 2019 weights.

These are model-derived time differences from scheduled service for unlinked train legs, not directly observed individual journey durations or door-to-door travel time.

The selection contains whole lines that serve Brooklyn. It does not isolate riders, trips or time spent in Brooklyn, and it is not every Brooklyn-serving line.

A person transferring between selected lines contributes multiple legs. Aggregate minutes are the modeled extra time on those legs; represented legs are not unique people or complete linked journeys.

total_apt and total_att already contain passenger weighting. Do not multiply them by passenger counts again or add this layer to runtime/OD-derived minutes.

Coverage is weekdays 06:00–23:00 only. Peak combines 07:00–10:00 and 16:00–19:00; offpeak is the remaining covered weekday hours. These windows differ from the end-to-end runtime dataset.

Additional times can be negative; retain signed totals. They measure net time relative to the contemporary timetable, not gross delay, total travel time, or a constant 2019 schedule.

Faster or slower scheduled service can change the metric. Fixed 2019 weights hold the line/month/period passenger mix constant but do not hold OD, schedule, train assignment or measurement technology constant.

In September 2024 MTA introduced a revised pipeline, including data-source and schedule fixes; scheduled-hold handling alone increased ATT about two seconds in MTA assessment. Pre/post values are not perfectly methodologically identical.

In November 2023 MTA lengthened fare-card trip matching; prior customer metrics were not revised. Fare-evasion scaling, representative OD models and data-exclusion rules also affect represented volumes.

Monthly totals vary with modeled demand and number of weekdays as well as service. Same calendar months reduce seasonality but do not equalize weekday counts, holidays, disruptions or other confounders.

No causal lane-conversion effect, mode switching, or corridor-specific net benefit can be identified from these comparisons.
