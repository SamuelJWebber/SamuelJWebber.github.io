LOCAL CITI BIKE SHARE CALIBRATION
Checked 2026-11-05

What is ready to use

calibration.json contains 57 DCP location/year/day-type observations for 2013–2015,
source links, original field names, counts, calculated fractions, reconciliation
flags, same-location changes, historical reference samples and the NYU audit.
dcp-local-shares.csv is the flat version for inspection/download. The other two
top-level CSVs contain same-location changes and the prior-study comparisons.

Raw DCP data are retained unmodified in raw/dcp_onstreet.json (108 rows) and
raw/dcp_offstreet.json (89 rows). Metadata and the official data dictionary are
also retained. SHA256 hashes identify the raw inputs used in calibration.json.

DCP definitions and audit

Keep years 2013–2015 and a positive all-bicycle denominator. Keep recorded zero
Citi Bike counts: six of 57 selected records are zero. A recorded zero is a source
value, not a missing observation, and cannot support a finite inverse multiplier.
It does not establish that the underlying probability of a Citi crossing is zero.

On-street share = citibike_all / cyclistvolume.
Off-street share = citibike_all / cyclists_all.

TotalUsers and AllUserVolume include other users and are not bicycle denominators.
No aggregation across different sites or day types is offered as a citywide share.
Ranges are descriptive spreads across published local observations, not confidence
intervals. No binomial interval is supplied because sampling design, day clustering
and within-day windows are insufficiently documented in this release.

The official dictionary states annual fall counts. Neither the rows nor that
dictionary supply exact dates or within-day start/end times for these 2013–2015
records. Those fields remain null. An older 2001–2008 report's 12-hour method is
not silently assigned to the later observations.

All 57 Citi numerators are nonnegative and no greater than the reported cyclist
denominator. Citi + non-Citi equals reported cyclists in 56 records. The exception:
Route 9A Greenway at 80th St., weekday 2013: Citi 4 + non-Citi 1798 = 1802, while
the reported all-cyclist field is 1802.5. The same source row has a 2-person gap
between cyclists plus other classified users and all-user volume. Retain and flag
the original values. Fractional published counts are not rounded or explained as
averages without evidence. Five on-street rows have missing gender component(s);
gender reconciliation is null there rather than filling the components with zero.

A concrete same-site comparison: East River/E Houston, fall 2013, weekday
298/1052 = 28.327%; weekend 65/454 = 14.317%. This supports keeping day type
explicit. The difference is not attributed to a lane conversion.

NYU correction and reproducible extraction

The report methods and Figure 3 distinguish electric and pedal Citi Bike. However,
Appendix C Tables C-2 through C-9 publish only Personal E-bike, Rental E-bike,
Moped, Pedal bike, Other bike and Scooter. No mapping from Other bike to Citi Bike
is documented in the inspected report. A Citi Bike numerator cannot be recovered
from those published tables. All Citi shares remain null; citi_extractable=false.

Source locations:
  Figure 3: PDF page 23 (printed page 12).
  Appendix C introduction and C-1 totals: PDF page 83 (printed C-1).
  Tables C-2–C-4: PDF page 84 (printed C-2).
  Tables C-5–C-7: PDF page 85 (printed C-3).
  Tables C-8–C-9: PDF page 86 (printed C-4).

nyu/extract.py parses all 384 device-count cells from PDF text. Table metadata
and the eight date/status/time columns were checked against rendered page images.
All 128 reconciliation checks pass: device rows sum to printed detailed totals;
detailed totals reproduce C-1; C-1 rows reproduce its totals. Five sites produce
eight detailed directional tables; the Queens northbound/southbound split is
combined only where the source summary combines it.

nyu/nyu-device-counts.csv retains device, delivery status, direction, date, period,
count, source page/table/column. nyu/nyu-local-denominators.csv contains 32 local
two-hour windows. Bicycle-labeled count sums the four bike-named categories;
all-micromobility adds mopeds and scooters. These are explicit label-based sums,
not a claim about how unpublished Citi Bike observations were recoded. 'Others'
includes uncertain delivery-worker status and is not labeled pure non-delivery.

Historical comparisons

Pulaski November 2019: 205/960 overall, 189/853 peak, 16/107 off-peak. Its device
definition puts throttle e-bikes with non-pedaling riders among motorized users.
Hunter April–May 2019: published Citi Bike share 27.1% among 4,325 observations;
no exact numerator is invented from that rounded percentage.
Basch et al., published 2023: Table 1 yields 318/922 among bicycles, or 318/988
among all listed micromobility. The roughly equal sample sizes per zone do not
weight streets by traffic. Observation dates are not stated in inspected methods.

These samples differ in geography, period, classification and collection design.
None is a current citywide multiplier. Direct all-bike counters remain preferable
to scaling a bike-share sample. Station trip endpoints do not identify crossings.

Reproduction

Use Python with pypdf installed for the PDF extraction:
  python nyu/extract.py
  python reproduce.py

The second script uses the Python standard library and the retained raw files.
Optional --refresh re-downloads DCP API rows and metadata before calculation.
NYU extraction uses its retained report.pdf, downloading only if missing.
The screenshots are provenance aids; regeneration of counts does not depend on
image processing. No Site files were changed by this work.
