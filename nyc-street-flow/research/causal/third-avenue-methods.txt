Third Avenue 2023: an executed controlled bus-time analysis
Checked November 5, 2026

RESULT AND IDENTIFICATION STATUS

The fixed-cell calculation finds a conditional 28.0-second reduction (2.74%)
in peak-period bus time over the 1.472-mile Third Avenue E67–E97 segment,
relative to the change on Lexington Avenue E96–E68. Actual fixed-weight time
on the treated segment fell from 17.149 to 16.543 minutes. The conditional
comparison is -7.7 seconds for AM and -45.2 seconds for PM.

This does NOT establish a clean causal effect. The primary control already
diverged materially before construction: its Jan–Mar to Apr–Jun 2023 placebo
change is +52.1 seconds, and its held-out pre-period forecast error is 54.1
seconds RMS. Those discrepancies exceed the 28-second estimated saving.
Spring 2023 transit signal priority, route operations, traffic displacement,
and other street-specific changes remain plausible confounders.

The treatment is the joint bus-lane, protected-bike-lane, intersection and
signal package. It is not an isolated bike-lane effect. The outcome is actual
bus in-vehicle segment time, including dwell and signals. It excludes access,
waiting, transfers and other modes. No all-traveler passenger-minute or total
person-flow effect can be computed from these bus records alone.

FROZEN PRIMARY DESIGN

The official design converts a typical five general vehicle travel lanes to
three while adding protected cycling space and an offset bus lane. DOT
announced construction July 17, 2023 and completion December 7, 2023. The
primary comparison uses January–June 2023 versus January–June 2024; all
July–December 2023 construction months are excluded. All 24 months of
2023–2024 are shown in the descriptive event series, with implementation
shaded. Later 2024 points are not added to the primary estimate.

The segment runs E67 to E97, one block beyond the E96 project boundary. It
contains most, but not all, of the project and must not be described as the
entire E59–E96 corridor. Included services are M101 Limited, M102 Local and
M103 Local. Weekday clock-hour bins are AM 07:00–09:59 and PM 16:00–18:59.

Each cell is an exact endpoint pair, route, service type, weekday and hour.
Month is retained separately. Counts from January–June 2023 weight cells,
and exactly those weights are held fixed in later months. Primary Lexington
uses the same treated route/service/weekday/hour weights. Each of the six
calendar-month pairs receives equal weight in the before-after contrast.

The outcome is mean minutes per fixed roadway mile, using each cell's median
pre-period roadway length. Results are also scaled to the treated 1.472-mile
distance for readability; the Lexington values at that distance are normalized
comparisons, not its actual 1.405-mile traversal times. We subtract the
control's before-after pace change from the treated change. Reported percent
effects use the additive parallel-trend counterfactual as the denominator.

Multiple floating-point distance rows within the same source cell are
combined using their actual bus-trip counts. Cells with more than 1% distance
variation are excluded; none met that exclusion. No approximate endpoint
matching or imputed outcomes are used. M1's Madison timepoint was not stable
across the primary window, so Madison estimates retain M3 and M4 Local.

CONTROL AND PRETREND SENSITIVITY

Combined peaks; seconds over the treated 1.472-mile distance:

Control             Conditional change    Pre-only held-out RMS error
Lexington (primary)       -28.0                       54.1
First Avenue              -32.7                       35.8
Madison Avenue            -28.5                       48.3
Third Avenue south        -26.6                       29.9
Third Avenue north        +53.9                       42.1

For the pre-only diagnostic, each control is indexed to the treated Jan–Mar
2023 mean, then predicts held-out Apr–Jun 2023. No post-treatment outcome is
used to calibrate or rank controls. Fixed January–June count weights and
balanced support are retained, so this is an outcome holdout, not a fully
prospective data-availability exercise. The primary Lexington basket has
complete support, so no post-dependent cell exclusion affects its result.

The primary control remains Lexington regardless of this ranking. No flexible
synthetic-control fit is attempted with only six pre months. Three validation
months cannot establish parallel trends. Madison's near-zero average drift
masks monthly forecast errors of +31.6, -67.2 and +38.6 seconds; it is not a
clear pretrend success. Third-south has the lowest held-out RMS error but
shares corridor signals, operations and likely traffic spillover.

First Avenue's control begins at E79 to avoid the directly redesigned
E61–E79 stretch listed in DOT's 2023 project archive, but upstream spillover
remains possible. The northern Third Avenue control includes only M101
Limited. Matching treated and northern M101 Limited with identical clock
weights still gives +54.5 seconds, so the reversal is not only route mix.

Primary route-specific changes versus Lexington are M101 Limited -18.8
seconds, M102 Local -24.7, and M103 Local -46.8. The combined estimate is
-9.0 seconds using Q1 alone and -46.9 using Q2 alone. Excluding the May pair
gives -21.1 seconds. These are disclosed sensitivities, not alternative
specifications selected to claim a favorable effect.

UNCERTAINTY AND COVERAGE

There is one treated corridor and six paired calendar months. Thousands of
source bins and bus traversals are not independent policy replications.
A descriptive paired-month t interval is -67.4 to +11.5 seconds; it assumes
independent month-pair errors and is not a valid causal confidence claim in
the presence of failed trends or spillovers. The downloadable data also
include a two-month circular-block sensitivity interval. Its apparent
precision with only six pairs does not resolve identification or prove an
effect. No conventional causal significance claim is made.

The raw download contains 35,660 rows. The primary combined-peak basket has
all 90 possible paired route/service/weekday/hour cells in every primary
month and retains 100% of selected published trips: 10,756 treated before
and 10,171 after. This is matching coverage of published records, not proof
that MTA captured every operated bus. The source derives arrivals from GPS
and includes coarse timepoint estimates, detours, dwell, operator reliefs
and traffic. Aggregated data do not reveal trip-level variance or passenger
loads. An independent standard-library recomputation reproduced the main
result to floating-point precision.

MADISON / OTHER COINTERVENTION AUDIT

The official 2025 Madison proposal extends double bus lanes south of E42 to
E23 and documents M2/M4 enforcement expansion in 2025. These changes are
outside the primary post window. Searches of official 2023/2024 project
archives and MTA/DOT releases did not identify a redesign on the E57–E95
control during January 2023–June 2024. That is not proof of no construction,
signal or operational change. The full monthly record is therefore shown.
The 96th Street redesign completed in December 2024 and later enforcement
changes further limit interpretation of the descriptive second half of 2024.

DIRECT CAR-TIME DATA

DOT's historical E-ZPass API for 2021–July 2024 returned HTTP403 with an
explicit login-required message in this session. Its current endpoint was
accessible. This is an access limitation, not evidence that historical car
data do not exist. No car-time effect is estimated or inferred from bus time.

REPRODUCTION AND FILES

Python dependencies: pandas, numpy, scipy; matplotlib for plots.
Run, in this directory:
  python fetch_bus_panel.py             # uses retained raw files by default
  python fetch_bus_panel.py --refresh   # refetches bounded MTA queries
  python analyze_third_avenue.py
  python qa_recompute_independent.py
  python plot_third_avenue.py

third-avenue-analysis.json contains estimates, 24-month panels, validation,
coverage and source evidence. CSV files provide the same comparisons,
fixed cell weights, primary monthly differences and validation results.
qa-independent.json contains the independent audit and route checks.
source-evidence.json contains primary-source URLs and a control-history audit.
Raw responses and manifests preserve API queries, retrieval times and SHA256.
