Causal design for NYC lane conversion: decision memo
Checked 2026-11-05. This is design advice and a literature audit, not a new estimated lane-conversion effect.

The exact target remains unsolved in the verified literature: removing moving car lanes for bikes, then measuring the causal effect on all-mode people throughput and total travel minutes. Five close papers are indexed in studies.json. Berlin is the clearest actual moving-lane treatment. NYC has a directly relevant taxi-based analysis, but its treatment is protected cycling provision, and its outcomes remain selected vehicle proxies. Three London papers examine related schemes/data and must not be counted as three independent replications.

The most executable design with our NYC data

1. Define the intervention as it actually happened. For Third Avenue 2023, identify exact installed dates and segments for bike protection, moving-lane removal, bus priority, loading regulations, signal retiming, turn changes and construction. A single simultaneously installed package identifies its combined effect; it cannot identify the separate bike-lane component. A route feature class or installation year alone is insufficient. Keep an intervention registry with dated official as-built evidence, partial opening dates, lanes before/after, usable width and concurrent projects. Never pool parking-only changes with moving-lane conversions without labels.

2. Use a corridor and a surrounding travel area. The local estimand is change on the treated corridor. The network estimand covers that corridor plus prespecified parallel routes and connecting cross streets between fixed east-west boundaries. Adjacent avenues are likely spillover outcomes, not clean controls. Estimate direct and neighboring exposure separately. A no-intervention donor must have comparable pre-trends, direction, signal density, transit service, land use and demand, and be outside the plausible rerouting catchment. First, Lexington and Madison need their own intervention histories; no control should be declared clean from its name alone. Carry sensitivity analyses across narrow and broad spillover zones.

3. Fit comparative interrupted time series / synthetic difference-in-differences for a single package, with an event-study plot. Use at least a full seasonal pre-period and preferably two years of consistent measurement, a construction exclusion window and matched post months. Jan–June2023 versus Jan–June2024 is a useful season-matched contrast, but only six pre-months cannot convincingly establish parallel trends. Match actual hour, weekday and calendar month; use pre-period-only donor selection and weights; reserve pre-period holdouts to check forecast fit. Show unadjusted trends, donor weights, pre-fit error and placebo intervention dates/corridors. With one treated corridor, thousands of hourly rows are not thousands of independent treatments: block time uncertainty and corridor-level placebo inference are more credible than naive observation-level standard errors.

4. Choose outcomes according to identification and coverage. Direct road probe speeds and fixed bus stop-to-stop times can be separate measured outcomes. Standardize each to baseline link/OD, direction, weekday and time-period weights so sample composition cannot masquerade as speed change. Report the raw mix and matched-support coverage alongside the standardized contrast. For speeds, compute travel time for each known-length link before aggregation; inverse of a mean speed is generally not mean travel time. Keep bus passenger-weighted and vehicle-weighted results distinct. Whole-line subway runtime is contextual system evidence, not a precise local Third Avenue travel-time outcome.

5. Build a person-flow screenline only if every mode is observed on the same boundary, direction and hours. Bicycle counter crossings are people-passings; Citi Bike trip starts are neither all cyclists nor crossings. Car counts require occupancy bounds and vehicle classes; bus counts require onboard loads at the boundary, not daily route boardings; walking requires counts. Subway station entries are not subway passengers crossing that boundary. A defensible total may therefore require excluding subway from a street-only estimand or explicitly modelling subway route/OD assignment as a separate uncertain layer. Do not sum several parallel cross-sections: the same traveler can pass each. Report people per hour over the whole corridor and optionally per fixed pre-treatment right-of-way width, not a changing denominator that creates apparent efficiency gains.

6. Measure person-minutes on a consistent spatial/time support. For vehicles moving within a network boundary, sum vehicle travel minutes multiplied by onboard passengers, plus walking/cycling person-minutes and transit passenger-minutes on included segments. For journeys, use a separate fixed-origin/destination metric that includes access, wait, transfer and egress. Do not combine these distinct accounting systems. MTA total_apt and total_att are already signed modelled extra person-minutes relative to schedule on unlinked legs; do not multiply them by passengers again or add them to end-to-end runtime and inferred OD totals. Annual system totals cannot be attributed to one street conversion.

7. Estimate totals and standardized performance separately. Actual person-hours can rise because more people make useful trips. That does not automatically mean greater delay or harm. Publish (a) people crossing the boundary, (b) actual person-hours within it, and (c) travel minutes for a fixed pre-treatment OD/time mix. Any induced/suppressed trips and mode switching should be shown separately. If passenger loads or occupancy are unobserved, provide transparent ranges and the assumptions under which the sign changes. Do not present a precise aggregate causal number when the needed components are unidentified.

Confounders and assumptions to make explicit

A causal total effect requires parallel counterfactual trends or a credible synthetic counterfactual, consistent measurement, no anticipation outside the omitted construction window, and a defensible interference boundary. Match/adjust pre-treatment covariates and external shocks: weather, holidays, school calendar, seasonality, construction, major events, transit outages, enforcement changes and congestion policy. Check treatment/control differences in return-to-office recovery, e-bike/fleet expansion and station capacity. Sensor replacement, sampling changes and missing observations can generate false effects. A clean pre-trend is necessary evidence, not proof that an endogenous installation was random.

Do not automatically control for post-treatment car volume, cycling, route choice or mode share when estimating the total redesign effect. These can be mechanisms. The London2025 mediation paper usefully distinguishes the direct road-space effect from the indirect demand effect, but that decomposition requires stronger assumptions than the total effect. Concurrent signal/bus improvements are treatment components when part of the package, not nuisance covariates to regress away.

Historical retrospective alternative

The Klingen approach can be reproduced or improved for verified pre-July2016 moving-lane conversions using geocoded TLC trip endpoints and metered distances, if the historical coordinates are actually obtainable. Restrict to within-road trips with route-consistent distances, keep fixed trip-length/OD/time support and report the fraction retained. Expand to area-direction outcomes to capture rerouting. Validate taxi sampling against overlapping independent road counters, allowing the taxi share to vary over time. Use a modern cohort-specific or stacked event-study estimator for multiple installations, avoiding already-treated units as controls. This identifies vehicle-time proxies for a selected historic sample; full all-mode results still require bicycle, pedestrian, passenger-load and occupancy observations.

Stronger prospective design

If choosing future rollout dates, randomize order among already approved comparable corridor clusters, or use matched delayed-start corridors with an agreed stable observation period. Instrument before construction: classified boundary counts, privacy-preserving occupancy samples, bus loads and observed travel times, all-cycle counts, pedestrian counts, and fixed OD travel-time panels. Treat parallel routes as part of each cluster, preregister the boundaries/outcomes/weights and publish raw aggregate panels and code. This can identify the desired all-mode effect much more directly than combining disconnected historical datasets.
