PURPOSE AND ESTIMANDS

1. Third Avenue: estimate the effect of the 2023 street package on actual bus
   segment runtime and, only if contemporary loads exist, passenger-weighted
   in-vehicle time. The package includes bus/bicycle lanes, intersection changes
   and signal priority; these data do not isolate a bicycle-lane-only effect.
2. Person flow: count all people crossing a stated boundary in the same direction,
   dates and hours. Vehicle counts, bus boardings and station entries are not
   interchangeable with motor occupants, onboard passengers or complete journeys.
3. McGuinness station 021284: assess motor counts at the verified detector
   location. Unless metadata proves otherwise, this is an approach-corridor
   outcome, not Pulaski Bridge throughput.

STAGED ACQUISITION

Request existing indexes, dictionaries, implementation histories and small samples
first. Use the coverage response to specify exact site/stop IDs, dates and fields
for a second-stage extract. Preserve the original files, retrieval dates, URLs,
agency correspondence and hashes. Do not authorize fees, infer an agency's
agreement, or submit requests automatically. Missing retained records remain
missing; the drafts ask for no new collection or analysis.

ACCEPTANCE GATES

A. Geography and treatment timing
   Verify fixed coordinates, direction, lanes and endpoints over time. Third
   Avenue E67–E97 is a 1.472-mile published bus segment extending one block beyond
   the E59–E96 project. Keep that distinction. Obtain dated work/signal/stop logs
   before finalizing the treatment date and construction exclusion. For Pulaski,
   do not substitute the bicycle opening for the permanent motor-lane closure.

B. Measurement validity
   Require documented units, time zone/DST handling, actual collection dates,
   sampling/expansion, missingness, revisions and quality flags. Check duplicate
   intervals, direction coding and channel continuity. A day with 24 values or
   96 quarter-hour rows is not automatically a functioning sensor day. Do not
   interpret unexplained zeros as real low traffic, or fill them by interpolation.
   Record valid-hour coverage by month and direction before comparing outcomes.

C. A compatible counting boundary
   Join modes only when boundaries, dates, hours and directions match. Keep nearby
   or different-date observations separate. Check whether vehicle totals already
   include buses and whether occupancy includes drivers. For bus time burdens,
   use onboard loads for the relevant segment/time; boarding counts alone cannot
   establish those loads. Do not count a transferring passenger twice at a single
   screenline or multiply passenger-weighted totals by passengers a second time.

D. Counterfactual eligibility
   Treat Lexington, First, Madison and the adjoining Third Avenue segments as
   candidates. Screen their project, signal, stop, construction and service logs
   for concurrent changes and possible diversion. Examine monthly pre-treatment
   differences and placebo dates using 2022 plus the uncontaminated 2023 period
   if retained. Use longer prehistory to test stability, not to select whichever
   comparison gives the most favorable post-treatment effect. Freeze the retained
   comparison set, windows and weighting before inspecting newly acquired post
   outcomes. Six pre months or a nonsignificant pre-trend test cannot prove
   parallel trends. If controls fail, report descriptive changes and bounds.

E. Weighting and uncertainty
   Hold baseline route/service, weekday, clock-hour and calendar-month composition
   fixed when comparing segment times. Report actual-mix estimates separately.
   Distinguish bus-run weighting from passenger weighting. Where passenger loads
   are sampled or inferred, retain their expansion and measurement uncertainty.
   Do not treat many trips on one treated corridor as independent interventions.
   Account for temporal dependence; show month/day-level sensitivity and control
   sensitivity. With few intervention units, avoid confident causal p-values.

DECISION RULES

- All gates supported: estimate the explicitly scoped package/segment effect with
  uncertainty, sensitivity and remaining assumptions. A measured segment effect
  is not automatically a whole-journey or total-network effect.
- Operations records available but loads absent: report bus-run time changes;
  passenger-minute totals remain unidentified or explicitly bounded.
- Aligned mode counts incomplete: report each observed mode separately. Apply
  occupancy, missing-mode and counterfactual bounds with labeled restrictions.
  Report a sign only if the entire justified interval lies on one side of zero.
  A scenario range selected by the analyst is not a data-established bound.
- Sensor relocation, treatment date, quality or counterfactual unresolved: retain
  a descriptive result, downgrade the causal claim, and identify the failed gate.

GAPS THESE REQUESTS CANNOT REMOVE BY THEMSELVES

Onboard bus time excludes access, waiting, transfers and egress. Road detectors
exclude travelers using other streets, rail or ferries and do not identify who
changed modes. Repeated aggregate counts do not follow the same people or reveal
newly enabled, suppressed or rerouted journeys. PPW bus relocation may move a
passenger to 7th Avenue without reducing travel across a wider Park Slope boundary.

A complete all-traveler claim still needs a defined network boundary with aligned
walking, cycling, motor-occupant and transit passenger observations, plus a credible
counterfactual. Whole-journey time and switching require an existing suitable
linked traveler study or a separately designed prospective survey, with comparable
origins, destinations, departure times and purposes. These requests neither
create that survey nor convert the NYC resident-only mobility diary into a panel
of all corridor users. No personal identifiers are requested.
