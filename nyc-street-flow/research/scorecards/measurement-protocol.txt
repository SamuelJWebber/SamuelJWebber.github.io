NYC STREET FLOW — STUDY PROTOCOL FOR ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE
Author: Samuel Webber
Prepared: November 5, 2026

STATUS
This plan extends analyses already inspected. It is a proposed protocol for
future collection and newly acquired records, not a retroactive preregistration.
Its criteria are proposed design decisions, not proof of identification.

PRIMARY QUESTIONS
1. Does a verified moving-car-lane conversion change the number of people crossing
   a prespecified network boundary per hour, by direction and period?
2. Does the conversion change complete journey time for a comparable traveler
   population, allowing people to change route or mode?
3. What happens to total person-minutes, trip numbers, and the distribution of
   travel times? A decline in trips cannot automatically count as a time benefit.

Do not infer capacity from realized flow. Demand, diversions and trip suppression
can change realized use. Do not turn a segment-speed result into door-to-door
time, or a before/after difference into a causal effect without a counterfactual.

SCOPE AND TREATMENT REGISTER
- Verify moving-lane removal from before/after drawings and dated work records.
  Protected-lane installation alone does not establish that a car lane was lost.
- Register project geometry, direction, lane counts, construction stages, actual
  permanent lane-closure date and dates of other policy changes.
- Treat bus, signal, parking, intersection and bike changes delivered together
  as a package unless a credible separate design can identify components.
- Prespecify street, nearby-alternative and wider-network boundaries. A wider
  boundary can capture diversion but must avoid double-counting the same journey
  at nested screenlines. Report nested totals separately, never add them.
- Register eligible treated projects and candidate comparison corridors before
  looking at new post-treatment results. Document all exclusions and reasons.

OUTCOME A: PERSON THROUGHPUT
For each timestamped interval and direction, estimate:
  pedestrians + cyclists + non-bus motor occupants + bus occupants + rail/ferry
  occupants crossing the same defined network boundary.
Keep drivers explicitly defined. If a source includes bus drivers in bus totals,
do not add them again. Do not add vehicles to occupants. Do not sum boardings,
station entries or train legs as if they were screenline passengers.

Use a fixed, documented treatment of movements that cross a boundary more than
once. Distinguish crossing events from unique journeys. Directional counts should
not silently cancel opposing movements. For aggregate totals, retain direction
and disclose whether one journey can produce repeated crossings.

The minimum compatible count record contains geometry/site, direction, actual
collection date and interval, mode/class, observed or expanded count, valid
coverage, source method, expansion factor and quality flags. Keep missing counts
as missing, not zero. Occupancy samples need vehicle class, date/hour, direction,
sample size, weights and uncertainty. Bus/rail loads must refer to the segment
that crosses the boundary, not merely stops somewhere on the service.

OUTCOME B: COMPLETE JOURNEY TIME
Recruit or acquire a suitable repeated travel diary / consented journey sample.
Register origin and destination zones, purpose, weekday and departure-period
strata before treatment. Hold that composition fixed for the primary mean while
allowing route and mode changes. Report actual-mix results separately.

Elapsed time runs from departure at the origin to arrival at the destination.
Include walking, dock access, waiting, parking, transfers, travel and egress.
Link legs into journeys and count each journey once. Do not assume absent access
time is zero. Retain complete and incomplete journeys, elapsed-time completeness,
survey weights, panel retention and reasons for missingness. Analyze nonresponse
and attrition; a balanced complete-case sample alone may select easier journeys.

A stable person panel can observe mode switches, but a traveler taking several
trips contributes several journeys. Repeated cross sections can estimate changes
for fixed strata but cannot identify who personally switched mode.

OUTCOME C: PERSON-MINUTES AND TRIP COUNTS
Compute sum(weighted complete journey minutes) and sum(weighted journeys) for the
same represented population and period. Report the weighted mean as their ratio.
Report a fixed-trip-composition counterfactual alongside the actual totals.
If trip numbers change, decompose composition/count change from within-stratum
time change. Identify newly enabled, forgone and rerouted trips separately when
observed; canceled trips are not automatically welfare gains.

The scenario calculator in this dashboard is an accounting model. It cannot
provide empirical mode-switch rates or causal travel times. Subway switching can
reduce or increase complete journey time depending on access, waits, transfers
and crowding. Add those effects once, in the same units and traveler population.

COLLECTION CALENDAR AND COVERAGE
- Seek at least two seasonal cycles of usable prehistory and a full post cycle
  where feasible. These are planning targets, not universal sufficiency rules.
- Determine sample size and duration using baseline variance, correlation by
  corridor/day, anticipated missingness and a prespecified meaningful effect.
  If available historical data cannot detect that effect, report the limitation.
- Match time zone and DST explicitly; use timestamp offsets and interval lengths.
  Do not equate a day with 24 values to a functioning sensor day.
- Prespecify valid interval/day criteria. Publish flagged, unflagged and missing
  coverage; investigate extended zeros, sensor moves and direction-code changes.
- Collect all modes simultaneously where possible. If proxy substitution is
  necessary, publish its calibration sample, uncertainty and transfer assumption.

COUNTERFACTUAL AND ANALYSIS LOCK
1. Define eligibility by road role, geography, transit access and stable
   measurement, not by the sign of the post estimate.
2. Screen candidate controls for their own projects, service changes and likely
   exposure to rerouted travelers. Nearby diversion routes are outcomes as well
   as possible controls; do not automatically call them untreated.
3. Reserve contiguous pre-treatment blocks for validation. Fit on earlier blocks;
   validate on reserved blocks without tuning to their outcome. Display all
   eligible donors and their common support.
4. Prespecify tolerance relative to a meaningful effect, not just a nonsignificant
   pre-trend test. Report placebo effects, held-out error and calendar stability.
5. Freeze new-record windows, weights, donor set and exclusions before inspecting
   their post outcomes. Use fixed pre-treatment route/day/hour/month weights for
   the primary composition-controlled result; report actual-mix results too.
6. Retain direction, peak/off-peak and project-level results. Across many projects,
   use timing-aware comparisons; do not use already exposed corridors as untreated
   controls. Aggregate with prespecified target-population weights and acknowledge
   that a mean can hide positive and negative project effects.

UNCERTAINTY AND SENSITIVITY
- Carry count expansion, occupancy and load-sampling uncertainty through totals.
- Respect the assignment unit and temporal dependence. Thousands of runs on one
  corridor are not thousands of independent lane conversions.
- Show donor, baseline, event-window, missingness and measurement sensitivity.
- Report medians and upper percentiles only for populations with underlying time
  observations; do not infer a distribution from monthly means.
- A range of analyst-chosen assumptions is not a confidence interval or an
  empirical bound. Call a bound data-supported only when its restrictions are
  justified. If missing modes can reverse a sign, quantify the threshold and
  show whether any credible measurements restrict them beyond that threshold.
- Reserve a final holdout or independent validation when enough data exist.
  Publish failed checks and changes to the plan as well as successful estimates.

ACCEPTANCE RULES
A. Treatment date / measurement continuity unresolved: descriptive findings only.
B. Missing aligned mode counts: report covered modes; no total-flow causal claim.
C. Segment time without passenger loads: bus-run or vehicle-run effect only.
D. Loads without complete journeys: segment passenger-minutes only.
E. Valid complete journeys without an eligible comparison: observed time change,
   not the causal effect of the conversion.
F. All relevant gates supported: estimate the explicitly scoped effect, with
   uncertainty and residual assumptions. A positive corridor result is not a
   universal claim about every NYC bike lane.

DATA PRODUCTS
Companion measurement-data-contract.csv defines recommended tables and fields.
Preserve immutable raw responses, retrieval dates, versions and file hashes.
Release reproducible transformations, dictionaries, QC exclusions and aggregate
outputs. Keep linkage identifiers pseudonymous; do not publish direct personal
identifiers or detailed traces that identify travelers. These are study-design
requirements, not a request to collect data without consent or agency approval.
